Savings Calculator Logic & Flow (HLD & LLD)

This document outlines the complete calculation logic and data flow for both the legacy (savings-calculator) and new (savings-calculator-new) savings calculators. Both services share a similar fundamental architecture, with the new calculator incorporating additional granular checks (like FPPA handling and specific sub-category mappings) and default resource lookups.

High-Level Design (HLD) Flow

The HLD illustrates the macroscopic flow of how user data and market data are ingested, processed, and served back as optimized savings numbers.

Low-Level Design (LLD) Flow

The LLD breaks down the heavy-lifting function (calculateSavingsHV2), which processes the core logic.

Complete Calculation Logic & Steps

1. Initialization and Data Retrieval

  • Inputs: Sanctioned Load, State, Discom, Consumer Category, Voltage Level, TOD (Time of Day) Consumptions, margins (Trader, ProLT, Probus, Consultancy).
  • Slot Capacity Calculation: The maximum energy tradeable per 15-minute slot is strictly limited by the sanctioned load. This directly enforces the rules:
    • "0.9 of sanctioned load bid hoga": MW = (Sanctioned Load * 0.9) / 1000
    • "Decimal ke ek place tak he hoga": The MW value is strictly floored to 1 decimal place (e.g., 1.35 MW → 1.3 MW).
    • Max Energy Per Slot = Floored MW * 1000 * 0.25 hours
  • Billing Dates: Standard month (1st to end-of-month) unless specific Discoms are detected (e.g., NPCL runs from the 19th to the 18th of the next month).

2. Market and Tariff Data Fetching

  • Tariffs: Fetches the state tariff based on state, discom, category, and supply voltage.
  • Charges & Losses:
    • stuLossPercent and wheelingLossPercent from State Charges.
    • ISTS (Inter-State Transmission System) Charges.
    • FPPA (Fuel and Power Purchase Agreement) Charges based on the query month.
  • Market Clearing Prices (MCP): Executes a FULL OUTER JOIN SQL query across DamRecord, RtmRecord, and GdamRecord.

3. Slot-by-Slot Processing (Core Engine)

The engine breaks down the monthly TOD inputs (e.g., 00:00-06:00 = 1000 kWh) into 15-minute granular blocks. For each 15-minute block:

  1. Landed Cost Calculation: Determines the exact cost of buying power from the exchange, accounting for MCP, losses, margins, and statutory charges.
  2. Baseline Cost Calculation: Calculates what the Discom would charge for that same block based on the TOD slab rates and standard energy charges.
  3. Decision Making & Allocation: Compares the Landed Cost vs Baseline Cost. If the exchange is cheaper, power is "procured" from the exchange. Crucially, this enforces the rule: "Market se utna he lena he jitna coming days mein consume ho jaaye". The system strictly caps the Open Access allocation to the minimum of what is available vs what is actually unfulfilled/needed at the consumer bus for that slot. The rest comes from the Discom.

4. Aggregation and Monthly Overheads

  • Total Market Energy (kWh): Energy procured via open access.
  • Total Baseline Cost: Total cost if 100% power was bought from Discom.
  • Total Landed Exchange Cost: Cost of the open access power.
  • Total Discom After ProLT: Remaining cost paid to the Discom for un-traded energy.
  • Demand Charges: Based on peak demand (kVA/kW) multiplied by the demand charge rate.
  • Electricity Duty & Misc: Additional overhead percentages applied to the final bill.

5. Final Savings Output

  • Net Savings: Total Baseline Cost - (Total Landed Exchange Cost + Total Discom After ProLT).
